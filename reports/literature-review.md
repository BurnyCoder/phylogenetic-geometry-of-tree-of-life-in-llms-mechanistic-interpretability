# Literature review of phylogenetic structure in representations

Libor “Burny” Burian · revised 26 November 2026.

The scientific question is whether distances between animal **names** reflect topology inside broad taxonomic groups. A geometry measured for genomes, masked descriptions, lexical categories or geographic locations supplies a comparison or method. Those experiments use different inputs, targets and opportunities for shortcuts.

| Primary source | What was measured | Relevance to this study |
|---|---|---|
| [Goodfire, Finding the Tree of Life in Evo 2](https://www.goodfire.com/research/phylogeny-manifold) | Bacterial genome windows, pooled across windows and compared with GTDB branch-length distances. The authors report angular graph geodesics, a supervised angular subspace with clade holdouts and associations with nucleotide composition. | Motivates a graph and a probe comparison. English names and edge counts on a sampled Open Tree topology are different measurements. Goodfire's fitted correlations do not calibrate expected text-model accuracy. |
| [Cutinha, PhyloText Mammals](https://github.com/jadencutinha/phylotext-mammals) | Student repository comparing embeddings of masked mammal descriptions with a mammalian reference. | Directly relevant work using natural-language descriptions. A negative result there does not determine the outcome of a hidden-state name test. |
| [García Durán, organism representations](https://github.com/alexgarciaduran/llm_internal_representations) | An organism-oriented representation project with code and exploratory analyses. | A reason to avoid a universal priority claim. Our contribution is the particular within-group endpoint, controls and versioned panel. |
| [Park et al., The Geometry of Categorical and Hierarchical Concepts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01506) | A formalization of hierarchical concepts tested using WordNet categories in Gemma and LLaMA-3. | A lexical hierarchy and an evolutionary tree are distinct reference structures. |
| [Modell et al., The Origins of Representation Manifolds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18235) | Studies how conditional distributions of contexts relate to the geometry of learned representations. | Motivates distributional control comparisons. A single GloVe baseline cannot exclude all co-occurrence explanations. |
| [Engels et al., Not All Language Model Features Are One-Dimensionally Linear](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14860) | Identifies multidimensional representations, including circular representations of calendar concepts. | Calendar plots are descriptive checks of the plotting pipeline. They do not validate tree recovery. |
| [Gurnee and Tegmark, Language Models Represent Space and Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02207) | Examines recoverability of spatial and temporal information from hidden states for named entities. | Motivates entity-name readouts and out-of-sample testing. Its prediction targets differ from phylogenetic relationships. |
| [Hewitt and Liang, Designing and Interpreting Probes with Control Tasks](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1275/) and [Hewitt and Manning, A Structural Probe](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1419/) | Probe selectivity controls and supervised recovery of tree structure in linguistic representations. | Motivates shuffled targets and comparisons with raw features. Neither high in-sample fit nor global correlation demonstrates a dedicated mechanism. |
| [Geiger et al., Causal Abstractions of Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997) | Tests correspondences between interventions in a proposed high-level account and a network. | Specifies why the edited quantity and measured change must match. A donor prediction after an edit can already have been present before it. |

## How earlier experiments shaped this one

**A tree in a genome model.** Goodfire pooled Evo 2 activations across independently sampled windows from each bacterial genome. They linked neighboring species vectors by angular distance, using the smallest neighborhood that connected the graph, and compared the shortest-path lengths with GTDB branch-length distances. A supervised angular probe included a reconstruction term. The authors report a distance correlation of about 0.9 on clade holdouts. Its reconstruction of embedding variance is a separate measurement. Genome composition also predicted probe coordinates. Here, word overlap and co-occurrence are candidate shortcuts, and the reference provides topology. The closest implementation-level comparisons are the graph-distance, family-holdout and class-only experiments. [Goodfire study](https://www.goodfire.com/research/phylogeny-manifold) · [Independent capstone code](https://github.com/jake-yukich/evo2-mech-interp) · [Local geometry experiment](experiments/03-geometry-probes-stage2/experiment.md)

**Organism names and descriptions.** Cutinha’s mammal study compared masked Wikipedia-description embeddings with an evolutionary tree. Its positive Carnivora pilot association did not carry over to the full mammal panel. The project also examined masking, ecology and human attention. García Durán’s organism study compared distances across five kingdoms with taxonomic relatedness and shuffled labels. These studies motivate explicit spelling and popularity controls and measuring agreement inside broad animal groups. Animal-question benchmarks test another capability, answering taxonomy or species-information questions. [Cutinha’s results by sample](https://github.com/jadencutinha/phylotext-mammals) · [García Durán’s organism study](https://github.com/alexgarciaduran/llm_internal_representations) · [TaxoGlimpse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11131) · [IUCN species questions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02830) · [BAGEL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16241)

**Calendar geometry and causality.** Engels et al. identified weekday and month circles in reconstructions using groups of sparse-autoencoder features and tested their role in calendar arithmetic. Our calendar plots use principal components of name readouts. Modell et al. give assumptions under which local similarities can recover feature distances along a manifold. Wurgaft et al. compared steering along fitted curves with straight-line interventions. These results motivated separate visualization, distance and intervention tests. A recognizable projection alone cannot answer whether a model uses that geometry. [Engels et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14860) · [Modell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18235) · [Goodfire series](https://www.goodfire.com/research/neural-geometry) · [Manifold steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05115) · [Calendar controls](experiments/01-pca-shape/experiment.md)

**Hierarchies and co-occurrence.** Park et al. analyze lexical hierarchies. Nava and Wyart show how co-occurrence that varies with taxonomic distance can produce a coarse-to-fine geometry under their assumptions, and compare the predictions with WordNet concepts. Karkada et al. connect symmetries in language statistics to cyclic and other geometries. Tests of property inheritance also distinguish category membership from simple similarity. These accounts make distributional similarity an alternative explanation for agreement with an evolutionary tree. GloVe covers one corpus and one construction of word vectors. [Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01506) · [Nava and Wyart](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23821) · [Karkada et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15029) · [Property inheritance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22590) · [COMPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01963) · [Local baselines](../src/phylogeo/baselines.py)

**Metric learning and control tasks.** Poincaré embeddings learn a hyperbolic representation of supplied hierarchical relationships. Hyperbolic syntax probes fit a mapping from language-model states, while in-context genealogical tasks provide a tree in the prompt. Tree distance can be predictable from a supervised map even when a cluster-only input already explains much of the target variation. Comparing raw, fitted, class-only and shuffled-target conditions helps distinguish those possibilities. [Poincaré embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08039) · [Geometric tradeoffs](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/sala18a.html) · [BERT syntax probes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03869) · [In-context genealogies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08255) · [Control tasks](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1275/)

## Related evidence and open questions

The earlier search also covered complementary work. Results on homologous proteins, animal vocalizations and images use different signals and reference measurements. Research on lexical hierarchies includes retraining encoders, probing hypernymy and identifying hierarchical directions. SAE studies examine which geometric structures feature dictionaries preserve. These papers establish precedent for particular measurements or controls. Their scores and sample sizes do not transfer to English animal names. [Protein representations](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbaf047) · [Animal audio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22458) · [BioCLIP 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23883) · [Hierarchy encoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11374) · [Hypernymy in BERT](https://aclanthology.org/2020.starsem-1.10/) · [Linear hierarchical concepts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07886) · [H-Probes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00847) · [SAE feature structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19750) · [SAEs and manifolds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28119)
